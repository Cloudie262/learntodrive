Oil-Fouled Spark Plugs in Gasoline Engines

would like to share some useful knowledge about oil-fouled (oil-wet) spark plugs, a common fault that can occur in the ignition system of gasoline engines. There are several reasons why spark plugs can become contaminated with engine oil. Some of the most common causes are explained below.

(1) Faulty Spark Plug Seal
The spark plug seals are installed in the engine’s valve cover area. Due to prolonged exposure to engine heat, these seals can deteriorate or become damaged, allowing engine oil to leak into the spark plug wells and wet the spark plugs and ignition coils. This problem can quickly damage the spark plugs and ignition coils, so it should be inspected and repaired as soon as possible.

(2) Faulty Valve Seals
Valve seals are installed on the engine valves and prevent engine oil from entering the combustion chamber. When the valve seals become damaged or worn, engine oil can pass through the valves and enter the combustion chamber, causing the spark plugs to become oil-fouled. As a result, the spark plugs may produce a weak spark, which can lead to engine vibration, rough running, and difficulty starting the engine.

(3) Damaged Pistons
The pistons inside the combustion chamber compress the air-fuel mixture, allowing it to ignite and produce engine power. Due to excessive engine heat, pistons can develop cracks. Scratches or scoring on the pistons caused by problems in the lubrication system can also allow engine oil to enter the combustion chamber. This can cause the spark plugs to become contaminated with engine oil.

(4) Worn or Damaged Piston Rings
When piston rings become worn or damaged, they may allow engine oil to enter the combustion chamber. This can cause the spark plugs to become oil-fouled. Burning engine oil can also produce blue smoke from the exhaust. Two of the most common causes of blue exhaust smoke are leaking valve seals and worn piston rings. In conclusion, the above problems can cause spark plugs to become contaminated with engine oil, which may result in engine vibration, unstable idling, and difficulty starting the engine. Therefore, these issues should be carefully inspected and repaired when necessary.
